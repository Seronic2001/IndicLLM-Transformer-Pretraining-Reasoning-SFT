\section{Introduction and Experimental Pipeline}

The development of autoregressive decoder-only Transformer language models has largely centered on high-resource languages such as English and Chinese. Low-resource and morphologically complex Indian languages present unique challenges stemming from complex conjunct ligatures, scarce digital footprints, and severe tokenization fragmentation. 

In this investigation, we explore the comparative dynamics of monolingual pretraining and downstream symbolic reasoning acquisition across two linguistically distinct Indo-Aryan languages: \textbf{Hindi} (Devanagari script, higher-resource) and \textbf{Assamese} (Eastern Nagari script `\indic{অসমীয়া}', lower-resource). Both models are built entirely from scratch, utilizing 100\% disjoint pipelines, independent vocabularies, and zero cross-lingual weight sharing.

\section{Synthesis of Core Research Questions}

\subsection{Data Scale and Quality Divergence}

\textbf{Corpus Abundance}: Hindi web data from OSCAR, Wikipedia, and regional portals exhibited high density, enabling 723M tokens with an 18\% filter discard rate. Assamese web corpora exhibited heavy English/Bengali code-switching, requiring custom scrapers and OCR of SEBA/SCERT educational textbooks to collect 118.8M manual tokens. The discard rate was 32\%, necessitating strict Unicode block filtering (\texttt{U+0980--U+09FF}).

\textbf{Noise Profiles}: Hindi data provided diverse syntax across formal and informal registers. Assamese required intensive normalization of conjunct ligatures (\indic{যুক্তাক্ষৰ}), archaic orthography, and numeric forms to prevent vocabulary dispersion.

\subsection{Language Modeling and Reasoning Transfer}

\textbf{Convergence Gap}: The $\Delta \approx 28.67$ PPL gap between Hindi ($52.26$) and Assamese ($80.93$) directly reflects the orthographic complexity and compound character entropy of Eastern Nagari.

\textbf{Reasoning Transfer}: While Direct SFT reflects pretraining scale advantages, Chain-of-Thought fine-tuning unlocks consistent multi-tier improvements across all models:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Hindi V1 CoT: Answer $F_1$ $+45.8\%$ ($29.77\% \to 43.40\%$), Decomposed CoT score $61.34\%$.
    \item Hindi V2 CoT: Answer $F_1$ $+38.9\%$ ($29.19\% \to 40.55\%$), Decomposed CoT score $56.15\%$.
    \item Assamese V1 CoT: Answer $F_1$ $+27.8\%$ ($26.37\% \to 33.70\%$), Decomposed CoT score $33.68\%$.
    \item Assamese V2 CoT: Answer $F_1$ $+26.8\%$ ($20.54\% \to 26.05\%$), Decomposed CoT score $32.98\%$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Tokenizer and Corpus Factors in Model L}

\textbf{Fertility Impact}: Assamese exhibits higher subword fertility ($1.4426$ vs. $1.1858$ tokens/word). Each sentence consumes $\sim 21.6\%$ more context positions, shortening the temporal horizon of the 512-token context window.

\textbf{Byte Fallback Dynamics}: With \texttt{byte\_fallback=True} and \texttt{character\_coverage=1.0}, zero unknown tokens occurred. However, rare ligatures (\indic{ক্ষ}, \indic{ক্ত}, \indic{জ্ঞ}) occasionally split into multi-byte sequences, requiring multiple attention steps to parse a single semantic character.

\textbf{Textbook Curriculum}: Manually extracted textbook corpora provided clean case-inflected grammar (\indic{-ৰ}, \indic{-ক}, \indic{-ত}), which proved vital for grounding downstream relational templates.

\subsection{Empirical Explanations and Mechanistic Evidence}

\textbf{Inductive Bias of Positional Encodings}: V1 Baseline uses absolute positional embeddings, forming static spatial coordinate registers that simplify position-based template matching in synthetic prompts. V2 Modern utilizes Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE), which excel at continuous natural language modeling (yielding 33--36\% lower PPL) but require explicit intermediate scratchpad tokens to span relative distances during symbolic deduction.

\textbf{Attention Entropy Redistribution}:
We quantify the attention shift using attention entropy $\mathcal{H}(A_h)$:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{H}(A_h) = -\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{i} A_{h,i,j} \ln A_{h,i,j}
\end{equation}
Post-finetuning attention maps reveal a \textbf{30.8\% drop} in entropy ($2.14 \to 1.48$ nats) and a \textbf{+71.3\% expansion} in mean attention distance ($3.42 \to 5.86$ tokens). Models actively re-allocate attention from local syntax to distant premise entities (Figure \ref{fig:attn_composite}).

\textbf{Mechanistic Script Dispersion}: In Hindi, single tokens cleanly represent named entities (\indic{अमित}, \indic{सुमित}), generating sharp $(i, j)$ attention coordinate activations. In Assamese, compound names (\indic{বিকাশৰ}) split into root and inflectional tokens (\indic{বিকাশ} + \indic{ৰ}), dispersing attention mass across contiguous subword blocks and increasing sensitivity to positional drift.

\textbf{Negation Curriculum Generalization}: Models trained without negative samples suffered complete polarity collapse (0.0\% accuracy on negated queries). A 5\% disjoint-entity negation curriculum enabled Assamese to achieve \textbf{63.83\% accuracy}, verifying genuine polarity inversion.

\section{Conclusion}

This investigation presents a rigorous empirical study of monolingual Indic Transformer language modeling. Modern architectural components (RMSNorm, SwiGLU, RoPE) deliver consistent language modeling gains across both high-resource and low-resource scripts ($\sim 33$--$36\%$ PPL reduction). In symbolic reasoning, multi-tier evaluation exposes the shortcut vulnerabilities of direct fine-tuning, while Chain-of-Thought scratchpads and targeted negation curricula establish robust multi-step logical deduction across both languages.
